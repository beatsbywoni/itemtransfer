## Abstract

Researchers select stimuli and explain task difficulty with measures that are computed from the item alone, such as the frequency of a word or the computational hardness of a puzzle. Whether such measures hold for stimulus sets that were built differently from those on which they were validated is rarely tested, and it is unclear which statistics can be trusted for it. This article presents leave-one-family-out transfer validation: the relation between a measure and accuracy is estimated on some item families (item sets from one design) and evaluated on a family that was held out. Simulations give four results. Intervals that resample participants but not items contain the true value as rarely as 51% of the time. The usual bootstrap over participants and items is biased for rank correlations, and with thousands of items its interval excludes its own estimate. Trial-level prediction gains little or nothing when the effect in the new set is about half the effect estimated on the other sets, although the items are ordered correctly. Stimulus sets with two levels of a measure, or with a different range, limit what can be concluded about the form and the size of a relation. The procedure reports the ordering of items, the size of the effect and the prediction of single responses, together with the reliability of item difficulty and diagnostics of the stimulus design. Four lexical decision megastudies and three sets of knapsack problems serve as examples. The open-source Python package `itemtransfer` carries out the analyses.

**Keywords:** item difficulty, stimulus sampling, generalisation, external validation, bootstrap, calibration, megastudies
